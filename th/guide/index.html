刺青について

Tattoos, plainly

A tattoo is not illegal and it is not a moral problem — the rule exists because of a historical association with organised crime, and it is fading. Many baths now allow tattoos outright, some accept a cover patch, and almost all private baths are open to you.

What to do, in order

  1. 1

    Filter for the mark that applies to you

    Every facility page carries one.

  2. 2

    Prefer a municipal bath

    City-run baths can't refuse you without proper grounds (Local Autonomy Act, Art. 244); Shizuoka City's baths say so outright.

  3. 3

    Phone before you travel

    Policies change without notice.

Bring cash

Small municipal bathhouses and village baths are frequently cash-only, and the nearest ATM that accepts a foreign card can be a long way off. Every facility page lists the payment methods for this reason.

Take off silver jewellery

Sulfur springs will tarnish silver, sometimes permanently, within a single soak. Iron-rich water can stain a light-coloured towel.

Mixed baths still exist

A handful of traditional baths are mixed-gender, and a few others swap between men and women overnight — the curtain you walked through yesterday evening may lead somewhere else this morning. Each facility page states the arrangement.
