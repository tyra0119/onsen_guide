Minoh Onsen Spa Garden

大江戸温泉物語 箕面温泉スパーガーデン · Minoh Onsen, Osaka

Up on the rise at the entrance to the Meiji-no-Mori Minoh quasi-national park, looking out over the Osaka plain. The water is licensed as a therapeutic spring and is reckoned among the best in Kansai; they call it the spring of life. Five minutes on foot from Minoh station.

The facility's own site↗

Temporarily closed Closed since 31 March 2025, along with the Minoh Kanko Hotel. No reopening date is published — check the operator's site before you go.

Tattoos OK if covered

Bring or buy a cover patch big enough for your work. The group's shared FAQ says the big bath is closed to anyone with a tattoo — but that a tattoo which two 10cm × 14cm cover seals will hide completely is allowed, on condition the group's own seals are worn. They cost ¥220 each and are sold at the front desk. A tattoo that two seals cannot cover is not covered by this. One answer for all 69 properties in the group, which absorbed Yukai Resort in November 2024. (Read 31 August 2026.)

Before you go in

1

Undress completely in the changing room — Swimwear is not worn in a Japanese onsen, and there is no exception for visitors. You take a small towel in with you and leave everything else in the basket.

2

Wash before you get in — Sit on the stool at the shower station and wash properly. The bath is for soaking in clean water, not for cleaning yourself.

3

Keep the small towel out of the water — Fold it and rest it on your head or on the edge of the bath. Long hair should be tied up for the same reason.
