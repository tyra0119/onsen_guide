Kuma-no-tearai-yu

熊の手洗湯 · Nozawa Onsen, Nagano

A wounded bear is said to have found this one, back in the Kamakura period. At about 40°C it is the mild one, with a hot tub beside it, and you can stay in a long time.

No tattoo policy published

A communal bath run by the villagers, with no published rule about tattoos. Baths of this kind cannot refuse use without proper grounds, but what is posted varies from one to the next, so check when you arrive.

The bath

Said to date from the Kamakura period. It has a hot and a cooler bath, and is among the gentler ones in Nozawa. There is an egg-boiling cauldron. House rules of a communal bath: wash before getting in, keep the towel out of the water, turn the inflow off rather than adding cold water, and do not bathe late at night.

  • Sulfur — Strong egg-like smell. Will tarnish silver jewellery — take it off.

Getting there

About 25 minutes by bus from Iiyama Station on the Hokuriku Shinkansen. All thirteen baths are within walking distance of one another.

Before you go in

1

Undress completely in the changing room — Swimwear is not worn in a Japanese onsen, and there is no exception for visitors. You take a small towel in with you and leave everything else in the basket.

2

Wash before you get in — Sit on the stool at the shower station and wash properly. The bath is for soaking in clean water, not for cleaning yourself.

3

Keep the small towel out of the water — Fold it and rest it on your head or on the edge of the bath. Long hair should be tied up for the same reason.
