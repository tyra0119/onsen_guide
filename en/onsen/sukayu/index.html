Sukayu Onsen

酸ヶ湯温泉 · Hakkoda Onsen, Aomori

The first place in Japan designated a national health resort. Its Hiba Sennin-buro is a pillarless hall of hiba cypress about 160 tatami mats across, and it is mixed-gender — there is a women's section, bathing garments are provided, and 8:00 to 9:00 is women-only. A separate bath, Tama-no-yu, is divided by gender.

The bath

The Sennin-buro is mixed. There is a women's section and bathing garments to wear, and 8:00-9:00 is women-only. If you would rather not share, Tama-no-yu is divided by gender.

  • Acidic — Sharply acidic. It stings on broken skin and is hard on sensitive skin.
  • Sulfur — Strong egg-like smell. Will tarnish silver jewellery — take it off.
  • Iron-rich — Rust-brown water; can stain a light-coloured towel.
  • Sulfate — Traditionally used for cuts and stiffness.
  • Chloride — Salty; holds heat for a long time afterwards.

Before you go in

1

Undress completely in the changing room — Swimwear is not worn in a Japanese onsen, and there is no exception for visitors. You take a small towel in with you and leave everything else in the basket.

2

Wash before you get in — Sit on the stool at the shower station and wash properly. The bath is for soaking in clean water, not for cleaning yourself.

3

Keep the small towel out of the water — Fold it and rest it on your head or on the edge of the bath. Long hair should be tied up for the same reason.
